Who Is He

The Baby-Faced Assassin

Stephen Curry grew up around the NBA — his father Dell spent 16 seasons in the league as a sharpshooter — but was still considered too small and too skinny for major college programs. He walked on at Davidson College, a small school in North Carolina, and turned into a national sensation, leading the Wildcats on an underdog run to the 2008 Elite Eight.

The Golden State Warriors drafted him 7th overall in 2009. Injuries slowed his first few seasons, but once healthy, Curry paired with Klay Thompson to form the "Splash Brothers" and helped turn the three-point shot from a situational weapon into the foundation of modern offense.

Between 2015 and 2018, Curry won two MVP awards — including the first unanimous MVP in NBA history — and three championships in four years. A fourth title followed in 2022, capped by his first Finals MVP award.

Now in his 18th NBA season, Curry remains a full-time starter for the only franchise he's ever played for, still adding to a résumé that already ranks among the greatest in basketball history.

The Rivalry

Warriors vs. Cavaliers: Curry vs. LeBron

From 2015 to 2018, the Warriors and Cavaliers met in the NBA Finals four years in a row — the first time that had ever happened in league history. Every series came down to the same matchup at the top of the marquee: Stephen Curry against LeBron James.

Curry's 2016 unanimous MVP season sits right in the middle of that stretch. Golden State won 73 games that year, the most in NBA history at the time — and still lost the Finals, the only blemish on an otherwise dominant rivalry record.

Golden State won three of the four series — but it's the one they lost that's remembered just as vividly. Tap a year for the story.

Curry wins his first championship and first MVP award. Andre Iguodala moves into the starting lineup to defend LeBron and wins Finals MVP.

Fresh off a unanimous MVP and a record 73-9 season, the Warriors take a 3-1 series lead — then Cleveland wins three straight, the first 3-1 comeback in Finals history.

With Kevin Durant now in the fold, Golden State avenges the collapse, dropping just one game to Cleveland. Durant wins Finals MVP.

A sweep closes the book on the rivalry's Finals chapter. LeBron leaves for the Lakers that summer.
